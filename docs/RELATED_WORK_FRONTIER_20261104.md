## Defensible positioning after the historical suite and 40k B study

We test the usefulness of a fixed acceleration-probe representation inside an otherwise frozen MASAC joint-capture protocol. The historical 80k development suite gives **static 26/36, full 15/36, zero 2/36**, with three training seeds and the same 12 development maps. This is a descriptive ordering; maps reused for development are not independent held-out evidence. Identifying useful predictive information requires additional controls for neutral values, validity and saturation; arm names alone do not establish that predictive content was the informative difference.

The historical episode-level audit further limits the failure narrative. Across those **108 final evaluations, 43 succeed and 65 fail**. Of the failures, **61 have only a subset of agents ever enter their goal balls**; only **4 have every agent enter at some time without completing joint capture**. Historical `ever_ball` counts test whether **any** agent enters a ball, so `ever_ball = 12/12` does not establish that all agents individually arrived. Source: [Windows episode analysis](../results/masac_v4_windows_audit_20261105/analysis.json).

The separate cold-start, three-seed **40k static B-off/B-on study is complete**. Control finals are **[2, 1, 4]/12** and B finals **[6, 0, 5]/12** for seeds 1511/1522/1533. Paired count differences are **[+4, -1, +1]**, giving a descriptive success-fraction difference **+4/36 = +1/9**. The preregistered engineering decision is **`ADVANCEMENT_RULE_MET`**, supporting **planning a confirmation study only**. The absolute 8/12 development gate still fails in both arms, **0/3 seeds each**. Across 72 finals, zero collisions are observed; the 54 failures split into **43 without all-agent ball entry, 1 with ball entry but not all-agent eventual individual capture, and 10 with individual captures but not joint capture**. These are symptoms and finite-budget outcomes, not causal proof. Source: [canonical 40k analysis](../results/masac_v4_sync_40k_analysis_20261105/analysis.md).

B is **fixed, standard potential-based shaping**, with no time-varying curriculum or predictive-information intervention. Its result supplies neither a new 2026 invariance theorem nor evidence that dynamic channels are useful. The [scene/content audit](../results/masac_v4_sync_pilot_diagnosis_20261105/l0_content_audit.json) found that all 12 L0 development maps have `dynamic_count = 0` and `conflict = 0`. The separately prespecified [late-replay audit](design/MASAC_V4_POST40K_NEXT_MINIMAL_PLAN_20261105.md) is now **execution-complete**: it recomputed full/static/static-constant-fill-1 on 6,144 identical-input samples from the six static control/B replays, without policy loading, actions, training or sealed-test access. The [report](../results/masac_v4_sync_40k_late_content_audit_20261105/audit.md) finds full equal to static-constant-fill-1 on **349,849/350,208 probe tokens (99.89748949%)**. The remaining **359** differ only in peer clearance, all in control seed 1522; TTC, braking distance, reserve, flag and the other unchanged columns match between full and static.

This is a content-coverage limitation with a concrete provenance, not missing peers: all **18,432 agent observations include two valid peers (36,864 valid peer slots)**. In 349,849 tokens, the valid-peer minimum exceeds 40 m and the 40 m masked-slot sentinel is smaller; 359 have a valid minimum below 40 m. Moving-object validity is **0/73,728 slots**. Static boxes occupy **52,038/147,456 valid slots**, yet static clearance differs from 1 in only **8/350,208 tokens**, with valid-far values likewise capped by the sentinel. Boundary features supply the most varying geometric content in this sample. These observations do not establish actor reliance on boundary features, predictive accuracy, a training cause, the old full-policy distribution, or fresh final-policy performance. Detailed [feature summaries](../results/masac_v4_sync_40k_late_content_audit_20261105/features.csv) and [provenance counts](../results/masac_v4_sync_40k_late_content_audit_20261105/provenance_counts.csv) support the restricted statement.

A separate, protocol-first [offline near-goal actor audit](../results/masac_v4_sync_40k_near_goal_actor_audit_20261105/audit.md) reuses the same replay inputs and provides a boundary diagnostic, not another performance benchmark. Among 8,000 agent states with `4 <= distance < 10 m` and speed below 2 m/s, a state-dependent necessary distance bound rules out strict one-step position/speed membership for **7,359**; its low overall frequency therefore cannot be called ineffective control. Broad-band counts are 168/8,000 deterministic and 10,440/512,000 marginal MC memberships (paired MC-minus-deterministic difference -0.0609375 percentage points). Restricting to `4–4.4 m` leaves 802 states, of which 161 are still excluded by that bound; deterministic membership is 168/802 and marginal 64-sample MC membership 10,440/51,328, with no uniform MC advantage established. These are per-agent kinematic surrogates on replay, not team-joint capture or fresh final-policy rollouts; `0/64` does not mean true probability zero. The [JSON](../results/masac_v4_sync_40k_near_goal_actor_audit_20261105/audit.json) and [completion record](../results/masac_v4_sync_40k_near_goal_actor_audit_20261105/completion.json) retain provenance. Cross-arm replay states are not paired, and common MC noise does not make marginal samples team-joint trials. The next proposed analysis concerns dwell and reward decomposition in the **72 already saved final trajectories**, with no new training or policy rollout.

Prediction, multi-UAV coordination, capability representations, safety enforcement, and attention already have substantial prior art. Our narrow comparison is the content of a deterministic, observation-only probe tensor under matched network capacity and a fixed capture definition. The tensor is a short-horizon kinematic heuristic. It is not an MPC controller, reachable-set certificate, collision-probability estimate, or CBF safety guarantee. See [the 2026-11-05 gap update](FRONTIER_GAP_UPDATE_20261105.md) for implications for B sync-capture.

## Goal-reaching, dwell and SAC evaluation boundaries — 2026-11-05

The following primary-source additions support the proposed diagnosis of the 72 saved final trajectories. They do not supply a new contribution or identify the cause of the current failures.

- **Vasan et al. (2024), *Revisiting Sparse Rewards for Goal-Reaching Reinforcement Learning*.** [Full author paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.00324v2), **Section 3/Table 1; Section 4/Figure 5; Section 5/Figures 7–8**. The SAC study distinguishes contact/guiding formulations from a minimum-time formulation with per-step -1 reward and termination on low-velocity goal arrival. It evaluates both steps to goal and steps on goal, and examines initial-policy goal-hit frequency in complete interactions. Reward, termination and timeout/reset semantics change together: this is not an isolated terminal-bonus ablation. Its initial-policy goal-hit measure is not our replay-conditioned, one-step marginal MC membership measure; its task-specific hit-frequency heuristic is not a transferable learning threshold.
- **Farrahi and Mahmood (2025 preprint / PMLR 330, 2026), *Learning Without Time-Based Embodiment Resets in Soft-Actor Critic*.** [Official publication record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v330/farrahi26a.html); mechanism checked in the [author preprint, Sections 3–5 and Figure 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06252v1). Continuing SAC changes reset transitions and uses average-reward estimation with differential TD. Its Reacher success requires both position and low speed. Fixed mean/standard-deviation experiments show that noise effects depend on the mean; smaller variance can be better at zero mean, which the authors associate with low-speed success. This is exploration evidence from complete continuing interactions, not a theorem that stochastic evaluation improves capture. Post-reset transitions and the differential target differ from our time-limit bootstrap contract; the paper does not diagnose our critics or justify changing temperature.
- **Quartz et al. (2024 preprint), *Stochastic Reinforcement Learning with Stability Guarantees for Control of Unknown Nonlinear Systems*.** [Full author paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.08382v1), **Section 3/Steps 1–5, Equations 21–26; Section 4/Assumptions 4–5 and Theorem 3; appendix “Limitations and Future Work”**. The method estimates local dynamics, computes an LQR gain and constrains the actor's local Jacobian; Step 5 explicitly uses the policy mean for stabilization evaluation. Its guarantees require strong gain-estimation and near-optimal-learning assumptions; neural finite-sample convergence remains unresolved. These conditions do not hold merely because a policy is trained with SAC. Neither its conditional stability result nor its evaluation choice proves universal deterministic superiority or transfers to our partially observed joint-capture task.

The remaining gap is the implemented task contract, not an unclaimed discovery that stopping differs from approaching. Three-agent simultaneous position/speed membership, nonabsorbing individual membership, event-history rewards and time-limit continuation must be described separately. Full-state stabilization results do not automatically apply to projected actor observations. Our deterministic command is transformed and limited after the actor mean; it is generally not the expectation of executed stochastic actions. Consequently, the offline actor audit and the proposed dwell/reward decomposition remain descriptive diagnostics, with no causal, safety, policy-improvement or novelty conclusion. None of these sources establishes that increasing a terminal bonus alone would improve this frozen protocol's sample efficiency.
